Rung 5.6 registered a negative: the fractal dimension of the critical cluster came out 2.43782 against the 3-D percolation class value 2.52295, missing a registered ±0.06 band, and the miss was locked with a diagnosis. What 5.6 never did was compute the standard error of its own threshold — so that band was never propagated through the steepness 5.6 itself had measured. This rung does that, and adds the one control that settles it: measuring the exponent at a threshold that is known exactly.

The control that settles it — an exactly known threshold and an exactly known exponent

2-D triangular site percolation is the rare case where nothing is approximate: p_c = 1/2 exactly (Sykes–Essam 1964; Kesten 1980) and d_f = 91/48 exactly (den Nijs 1979 / SLE₆). Measure the exponent at the exact threshold and the locator leaves the experiment entirely, with no literature uncertainty at either end. The dashed ladder shows how fast the answer moves when the threshold is wrong — the same mechanism, 10× gentler than in 3-D.

An unregistered fork in the estimator — and it flips between lattices

With five independent simulation batches you can average the curves and fit once, or fit each batch and average the answers. Nobody had written down which. Rung 5.9 found the two differ by more than the systematic the estimator was built to remove, and picked the ordering its single answer key preferred. Site percolation has two keys — one exact. Bars are each ordering's distance from the key, so shorter is better.

Why this keeps happening: how hard the exponent leans on the threshold

All of it comes back to one number — how much d_f moves per unit of threshold error. It is not a constant of percolation: it grows with dimension and again with the dilution rule, so it has to be re-measured for every variant rather than carried over.
