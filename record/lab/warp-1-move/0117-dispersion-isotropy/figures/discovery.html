Live discovery — the bench is told nothing, and returns the law

A frequency sweep on a 64³ periodic tetrahedral lattice, along five directions, with no dispersion relation supplied. The bench scores six candidate laws against the data and reports which one it is, how sure it is, and where the finite lattice stops obeying it.

FIREWALL. A toy DEC scalar wave on a tetrahedral 3-torus. ω, k, dispersion, light cone, speed, isotropy name numerical properties of the discrete operator Δ₀ = ⋆₀⁻¹D₀ᵀ⋆₁D₀ and of least-squares fits to its Rayleigh quotients — never a real photon, a real light cone, or a measurement of c. The prediction-grade quantities are the dimensionless exponent and the dimensionless anisotropy ratio; the fitted coefficient is an emergent lattice speed in code units. c = 1 in code units only.
What "blind" means here. The only path from swept data to a stated law is discover_law(&[f64], &[f64]) -> Discovery — its whole signature is the two data columns, so no expected exponent can enter through a parameter. The candidate menu {k⁰, k^½, k, k^{3/2}, k², k³} was registered before the run; the winner is whichever the data's own log-space R² prefers.
