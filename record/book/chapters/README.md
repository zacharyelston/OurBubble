# Chapters

The chapters of **[The Container — the study guide](../README.md)**. Each is a data-true result built from a re-run gate, authored from
the [lesson template](../templates/LESSON_TEMPLATE.md) per the [lesson standard](../LESSON_STANDARD.md).

> **FIREWALL.** This is a book about a *computational-physics toy* — a geometrical simulation system.
> *warp, photon, charge, bubble, negative energy, spacetime* name features of a lattice computation,
> never claims about nature. Every chapter carries its firewall.

> **A note on the name.** This project began as a warp-drive question — *what would it cost to move a
> region of space?* — and that question is still one of the chapters. But what got built is a
> **container**: one exact discrete complex that many sciences can be pointed at, from the light cone
> to criticality to the quantum vacuum. So the book's frame is **The Container** — *one lattice, many
> sciences* — and "warp" is retired as a name for the project or the book. It survives in two places
> only, both deliberate: as the **physics term** (the Alcubierre warp drive, warp bubble, warp shift —
> prior published physics this toy is measured against), and as the **immutable provenance path** of
> the lab directories `lab/warp-N-*/`, whose names are cited by committed specs, evals and
> `PROVENANCE.md` files and therefore never change. The engine itself is **UniForge**.
>
> *(This note is repeated here, and not only linked, because this page is the built book's front
> matter — a reader who arrives through the rendered site never sees `book/README.md`.)*

> Chapters land here as the UniForge migration re-runs the gates (#7) and the viz layers render
> the figures (#8 / #9). The list below is the running index — add a row when a chapter lands.

## Planned — chapter 0, "the object"

**The skeleton already exists and is rendered.** Section 1 of the synthesis front door
([`viz/synthesis.html`](../../viz/synthesis.html), generated by
[`core/viz/src/bin/gen_synthesis.rs`](../../core/viz/src/bin/gen_synthesis.rs) — see its `ramp()`
and the `s-object` block of
[`assets/synthesis_template.html`](../../core/viz/assets/synthesis_template.html)) is an **eleven-beat
ramp** written for a reader who has met none of this, and it is the intended skeleton for chapter 0.

**The rule the beats obey — carry it into the chapter.** One idea per beat, plain words first and
the jargon as an aside, and no beat may lean on anything a previous beat has not already earned.
The object itself does not appear until beat 10, as the *payoff* of the shape argument rather than
as the opening premise. The beats, in order, each forced by the one before it:

1. **What is the least you actually need?** The calculus physics is written in assumes you can always
   look closer; no measurement has ever seen that — every measurement ever made is a finite list of
   numbers. Whether space itself is like the calculus is an old open question and **the chapter does
   not need the answer**. So don't approximate a continuum: start from nothing. Somewhere to put a
   number is a *point*; somewhere to put *change* is the *line joining* two points. A point and a
   line — not a shrunken picture of something richer, a complete world of its own on which everything
   that follows is *exact*. A textbook continuum formula, when it appears, is a **comparison**, never
   a standard the lattice owes anything to. Plain words first; `0-form` / `1-form` named second.
   **Do not reach for the Planck scale here** — it invites reading the mesh as a model of nature,
   which is the firewall's exact failure mode.
2. **Differences around a closed loop cancel exactly** — as integers, not to a tolerance. This is why
   the machine is trustworthy *before* any physics enters. (`d² = 0`, re-verified at generation time.)
3. **Nothing yet knows how long a line is.** The structure is exact *because* it is metric-free.
4. **Nothing has moved yet. Make time.** Numbers have been sitting still. Add a clock — ticks, all
   the same — and exactly **one rule** for what a tick does: every point adjusts its own number
   toward its neighbours', by an amount the differences on its lines tell it, carrying forward the
   change it has built up. Say plainly that this one sentence is the machine's whole law and nothing
   is ever added to it. (Short aside: the discrete wave equation `φ̈ = −c² Δ₀ φ`, stepped by
   `solve`'s `step_scalar_wave` — needle both against `core/solve/src/wave_solver.rs`.)
5. **The rule makes ripples.** Poke one point and rings spread outward like a pond. Nobody
   programmed waves; the rule did it. The live demos are this beat's evidence — link them as *watch
   the rule run*. End on the reader's own stopwatch question: **how fast is that ring travelling, and
   is it the same speed in every direction?**
6. **"How fast" needs "how far" — and no line has a length.** Speed is distance over time; beat 3 was
   *proud* that nothing has a length. Counting hops is not distance — an axis hop and a diagonal hop
   are both one hop unless something says otherwise. So sizes must be written in somewhere. This beat
   is the motivation; the reader should **want** the dial before seeing it.
7. **The metric is a dial.** Opens directly with the dial (beat 6 did the motivating). Set it naively
   and **beat 5's ring** comes out lopsided — faster along some directions than others; set it from
   the geometry and the ring comes back round. Gloss *light cone* the first time it appears: it is
   just the picture of the ring's speed in every direction. Measured at rung 1.5, and caught living
   inside a public demo at rung 3.5 — say the connective clause explicitly: the ring poked in beat 5
   is the ring this dial rounds out and the physics behind that demo's before/after toggle. Then the
   consequence of beat 1's stance: there is no truer geometry behind this one for the dial to be
   tuned toward, so the dial does not *approximate* a geometry — it **supplies** the only one there
   is, and two settings are two different geometries with measurable differences. Point forward to
   the arc that makes the dial the object of study (`bridge 8.13`–`8.18`: engineering stars and
   pricing what each choice costs in isotropy, positivity and entropy — 8.18's *entropy cost of
   exact isotropy is 3.771e-5*).
8. **Finish the ladder before choosing a shape.** A patch bounded by lines holds a *circulation*
   (walk its edge, add the line-numbers — the same walk as beat 2); a volume bounded by patches holds
   what flows out of it. Only now do the physics words land, and plainly: `A` lives on lines, `F` on
   patches, and *the loop-sum of `A` around a patch **is** the `F` on it* (`F = dA`). This is where
   2-forms and 3-forms stop being an aside.
9. **What shape should a patch be?** Squares look natural and are the wrong atom — a square flexes,
   because pinning its corners does not fix its diagonals. A triangle is rigid: the smallest patch
   that holds its own shape. A tetrahedron is its 3-D sibling. Hence simplices. One idea, no more.
10. **Regular tetrahedra do not fill space** — the old surprise (Aristotle recorded the opposite, and
   it stood for roughly eighteen centuries). The page measures the miss on its own drawn tetrahedron:
   `70.5288°` between faces, `5.1043` copies to close a turn, a `7.3561°` wedge left over. So every
   tetrahedral lattice is a **compromise**, a compromise is a **choice**, and a choice has measurable
   consequences. *Then* the payoff: the cube-parity construction → the stella octangula, two mirror
   tetrahedra that never share an edge, octahedral holes between them, chirality on the ground floor.
   Quote the repo where it says it better (`GENESIS.md`: the stella-octangula is *a choice of
   lattice*; `core/geom`'s builder on the tet-oct honeycomb and its octahedral-hole centres), and
   keep the firewall on the *matter* / *antimatter* family nicknames.
11. **One object, two sciences — the whole page in miniature.** Before the twelve-cell map, one worked
   hand-off: the same operator on the same mesh, run leaked (heat — a walk that spreads, `⟨Δx²⟩ ∝ t`)
   and conserved (the quantum walk — ballistic, `∝ t²`), which is rung `bridge 8.19`'s committed
   result and is quoted as the beat's evidence. Close on the hand-off: every cell below is this move
   again — same object, different question.

move between the chapter and the front door without re-learning the sequence. This note stays as the
