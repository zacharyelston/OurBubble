One tetrahedron is a whole world

The chapter's own questions, one at a time. The census, the six differences, the four face loops and the sum round the inside are all worked out here, in your browser, exactly.

Scope. A toy, still on a napkin: one tetrahedron, and every count and every walk on it worked out while the page is running. Nothing here is a claim about nature.

Use ← and → to walk the beats. Every number is in a table as well as on the drawing, so the page reads with the pictures ignored entirely.

The tetrahedron is always the flat unfolded net: the triangle ABC in the middle with the other three folded out from its sides, all four panels the same, every label upright, and the diagram never rotated. The dot D appears three times because flat paper puts one corner of the solid in three places. No colour means anything.

No script is loaded from anywhere, and nothing about your visit is recorded.
