The napkin world, in your browser

Four pages, one per chapter of the book's first world — the one small enough that every number in it is finger-countable. Each page follows its chapter's questions in order and works out the answers as you read, in exact arithmetic, with no floating point anywhere in a number you are asked to check.

← the book

Scope. These pages compute a toy: a small world built inside a computer on purpose. Nothing they draw or print is a claim about nature.

  1. Two dots, a line, and the first thing that closes

    Beats 9–18. Somewhere to put a number, somewhere for a change to live, and the first walk that comes home to exactly nothing.

  2. One tetrahedron is a whole world

    Beats 19–26. Four, six, four, one — and both coming-home facts walked in front of you, on the flat unfolded net.

  3. Make it move

    Beats 27–35. One rule, ten ticks, a total that never moves, and one dial. Step it or play it.

  4. The shape between

    Beats 36–46. Cut the tetrahedron at its six middles, poke the shape left between the tips, and watch the arithmetic leave the napkin.

Every number these pages show is checked, before the book is allowed to build, against the same arithmetic the chapters print — tools/napkin.py and tools/octahedron.py, exported to demos/data/napkin.json and compared value by value by demos/core.test.mjs. A demo may not show a number the napkin did not compute.

No framework, no library, nothing loaded from another site, and nothing about your visit recorded.
