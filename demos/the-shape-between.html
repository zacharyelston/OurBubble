The shape between

The chapter's own questions, one at a time. The cut, the crossing, the eight-face sum, and the point where the arithmetic leaves the napkin — all of it worked out here, in your browser, and the last of it refused rather than rounded.

← read the chapter

Scope. A toy, still on a napkin: one tetrahedron, cut open, and what turns out to be inside it. Every count and every table here is worked out while the page is running, so you can check any of it with a pencil. Nothing in it is a claim about nature.

Use ← and → to walk the beats. Every number is in a table as well as on the drawing, so the page reads with the pictures ignored entirely.

Two drawings and no third. The tetrahedron is the flat unfolded net, the same one the previous chapters used. The shape between the tips — and the two tetrahedra threaded through it — is drawn on a ring: six dots on two circles, each on the same line through the centre as the one dot no line joins it to, so opposite is literally straight through the middle. Its twelve lines are drawn once each and none of them crosses another, which the page checks before it draws rather than trusting. When the eight tips arrive, four of them have to sit outside the ring, and their lines in are the only lines that cross anything. No colour means anything.

Where a number cannot be written down in halves, this page says so instead of rounding it. That refusal is the chapter's finding, not a formatting problem.
