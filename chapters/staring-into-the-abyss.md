# Staring into the abyss

> **Scope.** This page is a primer: one scene from the author's own life, told in the first person,
> and the idea it left behind. The book this page belongs to is about a **toy** — a small world
> built inside a computer — and nothing on this page or in that book is a claim about nature. The
> zero on this page
> is a mark on paper, and the counting that follows it is counting.

<!-- beat staring-into-the-abyss.1 -->

I moved into a house one block from the ocean. Close enough that I was made to look at an endless
empty patch of space several times a day.

The scene was not new to me. I had lived on the eastern shore of America my whole life. But now it
was a regular moment: my commute home ended in a view of the water — a tiny slice of water and sky
with nothing else in it. It happened often enough that I noticed I was smiling when it did.

The void made me smile. We took walks together to look into it. Hold hands, stand there in silence,
and look at the Nothing. And feel calm.

Why?

## Less than the other direction

<!-- beat staring-into-the-abyss.2 -->

I think it took a few years of doing it to grasp what the void was.

For me it was a place of less than the other direction. Toward the water there was nothing. The
other way there were streets and lights and cars and signs and people — and right next to all of it,
Nothing. And that Nothing let my brain go quiet: it did not have to look at the things, the
happenings, the existence. Peace, it turned out, was something you could arrange. Nothing, and so
peace, was achievable by selection and planning.

I still miss it. We moved. It has been years, and I know now that I need to go back. I need the void
close by. I need there to be Nothing in my life.

Which is a strange thing to say at the front of a run of chapters about drawing dots, except that
it is exactly the right way in.

## Write nothing down

<!-- beat staring-into-the-abyss.3 -->

Because zero is what made the counting in this book possible — all of it, every table in the
chapters ahead.

Zero is not my favourite number. That is three. Zero is the number that carries the most weight, the
most power; if zero could make a claim, it would claim to be the spark. Which is odd, since most of
us think of zero as nothing. That is more or less its definition.

And yet with zero it all happens. Write a 0 on a piece of paper. You have just made one number — a
zero. So by writing down a nothing you now have one thing on the page, and one thing is a 1. Look
again: the zero, and the one you just counted — two things, and 2 is born. You only wrote the 0. The
others did not so much show up as burst on to the scene.

## One direction

<!-- beat staring-into-the-abyss.4 -->

There is no stopping it once the zero is down. There is no last mark: each one you count permits
the next, so infinity is not something anyone adds — it is what you get, unasked, out of the first
mark. And it runs one way only. You can always make another mark, and none of them unmakes the
first.

That, I think, is what the void was giving me at the end of every commute. Not an absence — the one
place where a first mark could go.

The last chapter left you at a blank page, with less than Eratosthenes had: no stick, no sun, no
ground. Here is why that is the right place to be, and not a poor one. Nothing is on it, and nothing
is where everything you are about to count begins. You are standing where I stood, looking at the
water. Draw the first thing.
